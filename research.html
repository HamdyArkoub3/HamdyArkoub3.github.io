Research Projects

My work focuses on multiscale computational modeling of materials behavior in extreme environments, with particular interest in molten salt reactors, reactive interfaces, and future fusion systems.

I use first-principles calculations, reactive molecular dynamics, and kinetic Monte Carlo methods to connect atomic-scale mechanisms with long-term materials performance in advanced nuclear systems.

01

Multiscale Modeling for Extreme Environments

A central goal of my research is to build computational frameworks that connect atomic-scale chemistry with mesoscale and engineering-scale behavior in extreme environments. In nuclear systems, materials are exposed to high temperature, complex chemistry, irradiation, and evolving interfaces, making their behavior difficult to capture using a single modeling method.

Reactive force fields, such as ReaxFF and emerging reactive machine-learning interatomic potentials, are essential because they bridge the gap between first-principles accuracy and large-scale atomistic simulation. These approaches enable Molecular Dynamics simulations of chemically reactive systems, allowing us to study kinetics, identify the driving factors behind degradation, and discover atomistic mechanisms that control long-term performance.

02

Molten Salt Corrosion of Ni-Based Alloys

My Ph.D. research focused on understanding molten salt corrosion in Ni-based structural alloys through a multiscale workflow that links DFT, reactive force-field development, reactive Molecular Dynamics (RMD), and kinetic Monte Carlo (kMC). This framework makes it possible to move from fundamental energetics to dynamic interfacial behavior and, ultimately, to longer-time corrosion evolution.

At the atomic scale, molten salt corrosion begins with adsorption of F− ions onto the alloy surface, particularly at Cr sites. Strong F–Cr interactions weaken local Ni–Cr bonding, which promotes selective dissolution of Cr atoms into the molten salt. This process drives vacancy formation, surface evolution, and the early stages of corrosion damage.

The broader outcome of this work is a mechanistic understanding of how alloy chemistry, local structure, and reactive salt environments interact to control corrosion behavior. By combining DFT-informed reactive models with RMD and kMC, I aim to reveal the fundamental pathways that govern materials degradation in molten salt systems.

03

Fusion Systems & Engineering-Scale Modeling

A future direction of my research is to extend this multiscale background toward fusion energy systems and engineering-scale modeling. I am especially interested in connecting atomistic understanding with continuum and multiphysics simulation frameworks.

This next step includes finite element analysis and multiphysics tools such as MOOSE to study transport, thermo-mechanical behavior, and materials performance in fusion-relevant environments.
