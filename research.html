Research Projects

My research focuses on computational nuclear materials, molten salt corrosion, alloy degradation, reactive interfaces, and multiscale modeling for advanced nuclear energy systems.

I use atomistic and multiscale modeling tools, including density functional theory, reactive molecular dynamics, and kinetic Monte Carlo methods, to connect atomic-scale mechanisms with long-term materials performance in extreme nuclear environments.

01

Molten Salt Corrosion

R1

NiCr–FLiNaK ReaxFF Development

Development and application of reactive molecular dynamics models to simulate corrosion reactions between Ni–Cr alloys and molten fluoride salts.

R2

Surface Orientation Effects

Investigation of how crystallographic surface orientation affects fluorine adsorption, chromium dissolution, and early-stage corrosion pathways.

R3

Electric Field Effects

Modeling how applied electric fields alter interfacial chemistry, fluorine coverage, and corrosion susceptibility in molten salt environments.

02

Alloy Degradation & Chemical Ordering

R4

Chemically Ordered NiCr Alloys

Studying how long-range and short-range chemical ordering influence Cr transport, percolating corrosion pathways, and surface degradation.

R5

Percolating Corrosion Pathways

Understanding how connected chromium-rich pathways can feed selective dissolution and accelerate corrosion-front development.

R6

Grain Boundary Corrosion

First-principles modeling of fluorine-induced corrosion at grain boundaries and comparison with terrace-like surface sites.

03

Multiscale Modeling

DFT

First-principles calculations to study adsorption, reaction energetics, dissolution barriers, and local bonding at reactive interfaces.

Reactive Molecular Dynamics

Atomistic simulations of dynamic corrosion reactions, interfacial restructuring, and selective Cr dissolution in molten salt environments.

Kinetic Monte Carlo

Mesoscale modeling of dealloying, vacancy evolution, corrosion-front propagation, and longer-time materials degradation.
