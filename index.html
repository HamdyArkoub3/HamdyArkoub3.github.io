Summary

My work focuses on multiscale modeling of materials behavior in advanced nuclear energy systems, with particular interest in molten salt reactors, materials degradation, reactive interfaces, and fusion systems. I use modeling tools to connect atomic-scale behavior with long-term materials performance in extreme environments.

Beyond research, I have a huge passion for the history of Egypt, and I developed a framework to connect its vast history together in one "multiscale" picture!

Research Themes

01

Molten Salt Corrosion

Reactive molecular dynamics and first-principles modeling of Ni–Cr alloy corrosion in molten fluoride salts relevant to advanced nuclear reactors.

02

Chemical Ordering & Alloy Degradation

Understanding how long-range and short-range chemical ordering influence Cr transport, percolating corrosion pathways, and surface degradation.

03

Surfaces, Grain Boundaries & Stress

Atomic-scale mechanisms of fluorine adsorption, Cr dissolution, grain-boundary effects, surface orientation dependence, and stress-assisted corrosion.

04

Multiscale Modeling

Connecting DFT, ReaxFF MD, kinetic Monte Carlo, and engineering-scale models to predict materials behavior in extreme environments.

History, Civilizations & Complex Systems

My interest in history centers on ancient Egypt, the Bronze Age, and the rise, resilience, and collapse of complex societies. I am especially interested in how civilizations respond to environmental stress, technological change, war, governance challenges, and cultural transformation.
