About

Studying materials degradation from atoms to engineering scales.

I am a nuclear engineer and computational materials researcher working at the intersection of atomistic simulation, molten salt corrosion, and advanced nuclear energy systems. My PhD research at Penn State focused on understanding corrosion mechanisms in Ni–Cr alloys exposed to molten fluoride salts using DFT, reactive molecular dynamics, and kinetic Monte Carlo modeling.

My broader goal is to build multiscale modeling frameworks that connect atomic-scale mechanisms to engineering-scale predictions for materials in extreme environments, including advanced fission and fusion systems.

Outside my technical work, I have a strong passion for history, especially ancient Egypt, the Bronze Age, and the ways civilizations responded to crisis, technology, climate, and conflict. I see history as another way of studying complex systems — human ones.

Research

Research themes

Molten Salt Corrosion

Reactive molecular dynamics and first-principles modeling of Ni–Cr alloy corrosion in molten fluoride salts relevant to advanced nuclear reactors.

Chemical Ordering & Alloy Degradation

Understanding how long-range and short-range chemical ordering influence Cr transport, percolating corrosion pathways, and surface degradation.

Surfaces, Grain Boundaries & Stress

Atomic-scale mechanisms of fluorine adsorption, Cr dissolution, grain-boundary effects, surface orientation dependence, and stress-assisted corrosion.

Multiscale Modeling

Connecting DFT, ReaxFF MD, kinetic Monte Carlo, and future engineering-scale models to predict long-term materials behavior in extreme environments.
