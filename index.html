Research Vision & Themes

Vision

Multiscale Modeling for Materials in Extreme Environments

My research vision is to build computational frameworks that connect atomic-scale mechanisms with mesoscale evolution and engineering-scale materials behavior. In computational nuclear materials, this multiscale view is essential because degradation often begins through local chemical reactions, defects, and interfaces, but ultimately controls long-term performance at much larger length and time scales.

01

Molten Salt Corrosion & Reactive Interfaces

A central theme of my work is understanding molten salt corrosion of Ni-based alloys for advanced nuclear systems. Modeling surface reactivity in molten salts is challenging because corrosion involves coupled chemistry, charge transfer, and evolving interfaces.

To bridge first-principles accuracy and longer-time kinetic simulations, my research develops and applies reactive force-field approaches informed by DFT. These models enable reactive Molecular Dynamics (MD) and kinetic Monte Carlo (kMC) simulations that reveal atomic-scale mechanisms of materials degradation.

02

Future Direction: Fusion systems & Engineering-Scale Modeling

My future research direction expands this multiscale background toward fusion energy systems.

This includes finite element analysis and multiphysics simulation using tools such as MOOSE to study transport and materials performance in fusion-relevant environments.

History, Civilizations & Complex Systems

My interest in history centers on ancient Egypt, the Bronze Age, and the rise, resilience, and collapse of complex societies. I am especially interested in how civilizations respond to environmental stress, technological change, war, governance challenges, and cultural transformation.
